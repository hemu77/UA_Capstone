        05 / WHAT THE FULL STUDY SUPPORTS
        A closer-looking network is not necessarily a better social model.
        Methods, tables & limitations ↗
        

        01
        Density explains part of the apparent advantage
        Giving random graphs each observed network's density closed 86% of the fixed-baseline-to-Global efficiency gap. Global was closer on the Facebook mean; the random reference was closer at school. This diagnostic uses target information, not blind prediction.
        02
        Rankings depend on the comparison
        Classical models remained competitive on clustering. The top-two gap was just 0.0037 at 40 nodes on Facebook. Neither one metric nor one network size establishes an overall winner.
        03
        Attribute scores are not causal preferences
        Political orientation led all person-level conditions on two scores. For Global it led 6/28 on Coleman and 1/28 on excess edge share. All graphs use one designed roster; politics and religion co-vary and prompt order was fixed.
        

        Inspect the comparative evidence
        These figures summarize the full primary dataset, not just the selected layers. Ranges are descriptive, not population confidence intervals.
        

        Only one of 24 planned contrasts passed the original correction: iterative US-Hindi minus US-English clustering −0.0453; familywise 95% interval [−0.0850, −0.0056], adjusted p = 0.0238. Significance persisted in only 3/8 leave-one-block-out fits. This is not evidence about Hindi speakers. Facebook records overlap; the school is one incomplete survey. Structural similarity is not behavioral validation.
