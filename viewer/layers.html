      What the full study supports
      A closer-looking network is not necessarily a better social model
      Three results from the full primary study, not just the stacked runs.
      Methods, tables and limitations ↗ (opens in a new tab)
      

      

      01
      Density explains most of Global’s efficiency edge
      Giving a random-edge generator each record’s density closes 87% of its efficiency gap to Global over the eleven records (86% measured from the fixed small-world baseline).
      A target-informed diagnostic, not blind prediction; the share closed is not causal.
      

      02
      The most sorted attribute depends on the procedure
      Under a permutation reference that holds the other two attributes fixed, politics is the most sorted attribute for person-level procedures and religion for Global (28 of 28 model–setting cells each). In a joint dyadic model, politics leads in 28 (Iterative), 22 (Local) and 20 (Sequential) of 28 cells.
      Sorting scores describe one designed roster; they are not causal preferences.
      

      03
      Rankings depend on how records are sampled
      With uniform 50-node samples of the eleven observed records, Global is closest on efficiency in 9 records; with snowball samples of the same records it is closest in 0.
      No single metric or sampling scheme establishes an overall winner.
      

      

      Planned contrasts
      1 of 24 planned contrasts passed the original correction: Iterative, United States, Hindi minus English clustering, −0.0453 (familywise 95% interval [−0.0850, −0.0056], adjusted p = 0.0238). It held in 3 of 8 leave-one-block-out fits.
      All graphs come from one fictional roster with a fixed prompt order. This is not evidence about Hindi speakers or human preferences, and analyses beyond the planned contrasts are exploratory.
